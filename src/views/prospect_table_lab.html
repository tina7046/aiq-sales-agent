{#- ── Change log, not rendered ────────────────────────────────── Same treatment as the group lab: commented out rather than deleted, so the record of what changed and the measurements behind it survive without taking up the top of the page. Nunjucks strips comments, so none of it ships. To put it back, delete this comment's opening delimiter and the closing one after the list. Don't spell the closing delimiter out inline in here — it ends the comment early and the list renders.
  • Tweak 1 — the decision control moved into the Prospect cell; the trailing Actions column is no longer rendered.
  • Tweak 2 — Why this prospect? is 15rem (was 22rem), still clamped to 3 lines.
  • Tweak 3 — Accept removed; the X is the only control, so the list reads as opt-out.
  • Tweak 4 — the account CSV’s own fields became columns: Rank, Tier, ICP Fit, CRM, Recommended Pitch, Themes, Account ID. No columns removed — that’s the second pass.
  • Tweak 5 — rows are now people, one per prospect, with the account’s fields repeating down its group. Record Type and Record ID added. Still n/a: Last touch and Owner — no source carries them yet (Source was on that list until Tweak 13). The 8 accounts with nobody listed (mostly the Avoid tier) hold their place as one n/a row each.
  • Tweak 6 — seven columns no longer rendered: Rank, Record ID, Account ID, Last touch, Owner, Themes, Record Type. Hidden at render only — every row still carries all of them, so one comes back by removing its id from _hidden in this template. 16 columns down to 9.
  • Tweak 7 — the X is 24px (--slds-g-sizing-7) instead of the 32px SLDS default, with slds-button__icon_small on the glyph.
  • Tweak 8 — the X is vertically centred in the row instead of top-aligned with the name.
  • Tweak 9 — headcount, industry and location now fill in under the company name. Note the cost of this grain: all three repeat on every row of an account, so Notion’s carry ten times. The group lab states them once.
  • Tweak 10 — email and phone fill in under each name, as mailto: and tel: links. Free here: the Prospect cell was already four lines tall, so rows didn’t grow and neither did the table. The 8 placeholder rows stay n/a — no person, no address.
  • Tweak 11 — the Tier badge moved into the Company cell, pinned top right and level with the company name, and the standalone Tier column is no longer rendered. Tier and company name are the pair you read together when deciding whether an account is worth the time, and they were four columns apart.
    It’s a net saving: Company grew 325px → 390px to hold the badge, but the 120px Tier column went away, so the table is 2243px → 2188px wide. Depth is unchanged at 4651px — the badge sits beside a cell that was already four lines tall. Nothing truncates at the new width.
    Two mechanics worth knowing. The badge is pinned with margin-left: auto, which absorbs the free space in the cell, so all 44 badges share one right edge; align-self: flex-start is what makes “top” read as deliberate, levelling it with the name rather than centring it against four lines of firmographics. And flex: none matters — without it the badge would be what shrinks when the cell is tight, and a clipped “Red Ho…” is worse than a clipped industry string.
    The fold is opt-in (cellCompany(row, true)) because the pivot lab shares this macro and still shows Tier as its own column; passing the badge there would print the tier twice.
  • Tweak 12 — Company narrowed to 320px, from the ~390px its content was driving, taking the table 2188px → 2118px. Matches the group lab’s Company column so the two are comparable side by side.
    Setting a width was not enough, and that’s the interesting part: every width from 384px down to 160px left the column at 390px. The three firmographic lines carried slds-truncate, which is white-space: nowrap, and a nowrap line’s min-content width is the entire string — “Software / Cloud Monitoring & Observability” is 265px of unbreakable line, so that plus the logo, badge and padding was the floor. No requested width can go under a column’s min-content, and min-width: 0 doesn’t help: that governs flex shrinking inside a known box, not the intrinsic width a table asks each column for. Letting those lines wrap — then clamping each to one line, which looks identical and keeps the ellipsis — drops their contribution to their longest word and hands control back to the width. The company name keeps nowrap on purpose: it never clips, and it sets the real floor at 241px (“Northbend Logistics” plus the fixed furniture).
    Cost at 320px, across all 44 rows: 11 industry lines clip and nothing else does. Narrower is available and the trade is close to linear — 288px clips 27 industries and 3 locations, 272px clips 30, 256px clips 42. Everything clipped is still on the element’s title. Depth doesn’t move at any width (4651px), because rows are sized by the Prospect cell’s four lines rather than by this one.
  • Tweak 13 — the Source badges are back. The column had been rendering 44 muted n/as, because neither CSV has a source column and every row fell to the macro’s n/a branch. The badge markup was never the problem and hasn’t changed — five variants plus a stacked New Contact / New Account, all still the shared cellSource macro.
    What changed is that build_lab_data.py now derives a source per person instead of leaving it blank, from what the account’s CRM and Signals strings already say about them: named on the opportunity reads as Salesforce, named as a new hire or promotion in a signal reads as press, an exec at an account whose opp is in Negotiation or Solution Validation is on the buying committee by then, a sales-dev leader at an account whose signals quote figures (“290+ SDRs”, “1,174 sales staff”) reads as a research document, and ops or enablement pain (“Data cleanliness pain acknowledged”) is the kind that surfaces on a call. No trace in either string is the closest this data comes to saying “not in the CRM yet”, which is the ZoomInfo + New Contact pair.
    Across the 44 rows: 15 ZoomInfo, 10 Report, 9 Gong, 6 Salesforce, 4 TechCrunch, with New Contact on 10 and New Account on the 5 accounts that have no CRM record at all. It is deterministic, so a rebuild doesn’t reshuffle the column — but it’s a judgement about which badge fits each row, not something the CSVs assert, and the generator says so at the rules.
    Free, dimensionally: the column was already held at its 152px floor by the header, so the table stays 2118px wide and 4651px deep even though 15 rows now carry two badges.
  • Tweak 14 — clicking a row no longer selects it. It used to paint the row --slds-g-color-brand-base-95, a blue that read as a selection the page had nothing to do with — there’s no side panel here for a selected row to drive.
    The behaviour went, not just the paint: the click handler, the is-selected class it set, and aria-selected / tabindex on every row. Unpainting alone would have left a row that announces itself as selected to a screen reader with nothing on screen to match, which is worse than either state. The cursor went from pointer to default for the same reason, and the caption below the table stopped telling you to click a row.
    Hover is untouched — the neutral grey still tracks the row you’re reading, and slds-hint-parent stays on every row because that’s the hook SLDS’s own row-hover rule matches. The X still removes a prospect and the tally still counts.
    The two .is-selected rules are still in the stylesheet, and deliberately: the pivot lab shares this sheet and still selects rows on click, so deleting them would have quietly un-painted that page instead.
  • Tweak 15 — Signal is a real bulleted list, one signal per line, instead of the whole set middot-joined into a paragraph that read as a single long string.
    The join wasn’t arbitrary — -webkit-line-clamp needs one inline flow to place its ellipsis, so block children inside a clamped box get cut off without one, and the clamp was what bounded the cell’s height. The list bounds height by capping the items instead, which is a better cut: a clamp ends mid-phrase wherever the third line lands, a cap ends on a whole signal and tells you how many it held back.
    Two bullets, where the group lab’s company cell shows three, and the difference is height the two cells don’t equally have. This cell has ~100px before it becomes what sets the row height. Measured: 2 bullets are 108px and take the table 4651px → 4687px deep, 2 of 44 rows growing; 3 bullets are 144px and take it to 5223px, a 12% longer page; 3 bullets clamped to one line each still overshoot at 108px and cost 6 of the 64 signals their tail. So the third bullet is expensive and the clamp that would pay for it doesn’t. The group lab could afford three because its cell was already the tallest thing in the row.
    Nothing truncates now — long signals wrap — and the full list is still on the cell’s title. SLDS resets list markers globally, so the disc had to be asked for back.
